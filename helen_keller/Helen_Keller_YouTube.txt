DESCRIPTION
Tuscumbia, Alabama, 1887. At an old water pump, a six-year-old girl who could not see or hear felt a single word spelled into her hand, and her whole life changed.

This is the full story of Helen Keller (1880–1968), from the silent dark of her childhood to Radcliffe College, from vaudeville stages to the halls of Congress, and from Japan and Korea to the ruins of Hiroshima. We follow her extraordinary friendship with her teacher Anne Sullivan, her meetings with Alexander Graham Bell and Mark Twain, and the side of Helen that history often forgets: the writer, the activist, and the radical who fought for workers, for women, and for millions of blind people around the world.

Most people remember the little girl at the water pump. Few know what the grown woman had to say.

In this documentary:
- The illness that left Helen blind and deaf at nineteen months
- Anne Sullivan, from the Tewksbury poorhouse to the Perkins Institution
- The battle at the breakfast table and the miracle at the water pump
- The "Frost King" scandal that haunted Helen for years
- Mark Twain, Henry Rogers, and the road to Radcliffe
- Socialism, women's suffrage, and the newspapers that turned on her
- A secret engagement, a Hollywood film, and four years on the vaudeville stage
- The American Foundation for the Blind and her letter to the students of Germany
- Her journey to Japan and Korea in 1937, and the first Akita in America
- The fire at Arcan Ridge, Hiroshima, The Miracle Worker, and her final years

All historical photographs are real archival images in the public domain, mainly from the Library of Congress.
